B2B eCommerce · Business systems · Process improvement

Operating and improving a B2B eCommerce channel across four connected systems

As the sole day-to-day administrator of the UK B2B eStore, I manage an operational journey spanning Adobe Commerce, SAP ERP, Dynamics 365 and ServiceNow. The channel processes around 120 to 140 orders per month, requiring me to monitor order quality, investigate cross-system issues and coordinate improvements without interrupting normal trading.

My role and scope
  • Monitor the day-to-day UK eStore order journey
  • Validate order, customer and system information
  • Investigate incidents that cross system and team boundaries
  • Coordinate evidence, escalation, testing and resolution
  • Identify recurring manual work and opportunities for process improvement

The examples below show three different parts of the role: resolving a high-impact incident, redesigning a repetitive workflow and preventing order issues before fulfilment.

Featured case / Duplicate freight

Tracing a duplicate-freight error across four connected systems

I identified a systemic freight error affecting 65 orders, traced the issue through the connected order journey and coordinated correction and escalation while normal ordering continued.

65
affected orders
15

Context, ownership and constraints

The UK eStore depends on connected storefront, ERP, customer-data and service-management systems. An error visible on an individual order can therefore originate somewhere else in the wider journey.

A third-party SAP application introduced duplicate freight charges. I established the scale, validated the evidence and coordinated the response. The technical correction itself remained with the relevant SAP and support teams.

  • Customer impact had to be managed proactively.
  • Normal ordering could not be blocked.
  • The third-party application was outside my direct control.
  • Other business units could potentially face the same issue.
01

Observe

Start where the friction appears.

Unusual freight charges provided the first signal. I compared the affected orders to determine whether the issue came from individual order entry or a repeatable system pattern, then established who was affected and which orders had already progressed to invoicing.

02

Trace

Follow the hand-offs, not just the screen.

I connected the storefront behaviour with SAP order and invoice data and the operational steps around them. This traced the duplicated charge to a third-party SAP application rather than treating 65 orders as unrelated exceptions.

03

Coordinate

Turn the diagnosis into a controlled response.

I documented the evidence and raised the incident with the SAP and global teams, highlighting the possible risk to other business units. I coordinated adjustments for affected orders, prioritised customer communication where needed and kept the normal eStore order flow moving during the investigation.

04

Prevent

Escalate the pattern, not only the individual errors.

In addition to correcting the affected orders, I asked the SAP team to investigate preventative action and informed the global team so that the issue could be assessed beyond the UK operation.

Incident-specific outcomes
  • 65 affected orders identified and brought into the correction process
  • 15 already-invoiced orders isolated for additional action
  • Customers informed and order adjustments coordinated where required
  • Normal eStore ordering continued during investigation and correction
  • Potential cross-market risk raised with the global team
  • Preventative-action request escalated to the SAP team

Mini case 02 / Pricing workflow

Turning a manual pricing task into a validated upload workflow

Key-account pricing updates required account discounts to be matched and entered manually before the data could be sent to IT for upload into Adobe Commerce. The repetitive preparation created unnecessary administrative work and increased the opportunity for input errors.

  1. Source pricing
  2. Copilot-generated CSV
  3. Human validation
  4. IT upload

I identified the repeatable logic in the task and configured a Copilot agent to generate the required CSV from the source pricing information. I defined the expected structure and checks so that the output could be reviewed before being submitted to IT.

My contribution and outcome
  • Identified the manual steps and transformation logic
  • Defined source inputs and the CSV format required by IT
  • Prompted and configured the Copilot agent
  • Checked account-to-discount mappings and output structure
  • Retained human validation before upload

The workflow replaced repeated manual matching and data entry with a generated, reviewable CSV while retaining validation before upload.

Mini case 03 / Order pre-check

Catching order issues before they became fulfilment problems

Orders could contain customer, product, pricing or processing issues that were easier to correct before they progressed into fulfilment, invoicing or a potential return.

I introduced a consistent pre-processing review across approximately 120 to 140 monthly eStore orders. When something did not align with the available customer, product or system information, I investigated and coordinated the correction before the order moved forward.

  • Customer information
  • Product details
  • Pricing
  • Processing exceptions

Approximately 10% of monthly orders are corrected during pre-processing checks, reducing the risk of avoidable fulfilment, invoicing and return issues later in the order journey.

My role in the review

I compare incoming orders with relevant records, clarify exceptions, coordinate corrections with internal teams or customers, and capture recurring patterns for wider follow-up.

The thread

The medium changed.
The underlying practice did not.

Across flowers, spaces and systems, I return to the same working practice: understand the whole situation, give ambiguity a useful structure and stay accountable until the result works.

01

See the whole picture

Observe closely. Understand the relationships between people, systems, space and information before acting.

02

Give ambiguity a structure

Explore what could be, make a judgement and translate an unclear need into a brief, process, journey or plan that others can use.

03

Make it real

Carry it through. Coordinate the details, test the result and stay accountable until the experience works. Then look again.
