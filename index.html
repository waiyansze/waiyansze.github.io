Digital operations · Business systems · Process improvement

Turning operational friction into a more reliable B2B eCommerce channel

As the sole day-to-day administrator of the UK B2B eStore, I manage an order journey spanning Adobe Commerce, SAP ERP, Dynamics 365 and ServiceNow. Across approximately 120 to 140 orders each month, my role is to spot operational friction, understand where it originates and coordinate practical improvements without interrupting normal trading.

Context, my role and approach

Context. The eStore is not a standalone website. Every order depends on customer records, pricing, product data, fulfilment and invoicing moving correctly between systems and teams. A small discrepancy at one point can surface downstream as an incorrect charge, a failed order status, additional customer contact or avoidable manual work.

My role. I sit between customers, commercial operations and technical teams. I validate what happened, connect evidence across systems, distinguish isolated exceptions from repeatable patterns and translate operational problems into actions that the relevant team can investigate, test or implement.

The improvement approach. Rather than treating every question or ticket as a separate task, I look across order checks, customer feedback, system data, analytics and service requests to find the underlying pattern. The aim is not only to correct the immediate issue, but to make the channel easier to operate and less likely to create the same friction again.

Operating scope. Approximately 120 to 140 orders reviewed per month; 98 incidents and 36 service requests coordinated.

Stakeholders. Customers, Sales, Customer Service, Finance, Logistics, Warehouse, Product, IT and technical support.

Four examples of one improvement practice: quality control, automation, cross-system diagnosis and operational visibility.

01 / Catch issues early

Make quality control part of the journey.

I review approximately 120 to 140 eStore orders each month before they progress through normal processing. When customer, product, pricing or system information does not align, I investigate the exception and coordinate the correction at the earliest practical point. Around 10% of monthly orders currently require some form of pre-processing correction, reducing the risk of avoidable fulfilment, invoicing or return issues downstream.

  • Customer information
  • Product details
  • Pricing
  • System information

Illustrative review areas, not a reproduced internal checklist.

  • ~10%of monthly orders corrected before normal processing

02 / Remove repetitive work

Turn repeatable logic into a controlled workflow.

Key-account pricing updates previously required discounts to be matched and entered manually before IT could upload them into Adobe Commerce. I identified the repeatable logic, configured a Copilot agent to generate the required CSV and retained human checks on the account mappings and output structure before submission. The resulting workflow replaces repeated data entry with a faster, reviewable process.

  3. Human validation
  4. IT upload

03 / Trace connected failures

Follow the hand-offs, not only the visible error.

When a third-party SAP application introduced duplicate freight charges, I established that the problem affected 65 orders, including 15 that had already been invoiced. I connected the order and invoice evidence, raised the incident with the SAP and global teams and coordinated customer and order corrections while the normal eStore flow continued. The incident became one example of a wider principle: resolve the immediate impact, then escalate the pattern so it can be assessed beyond a single order or market.

04 / Restore operational visibility

Make the channel easier to understand and manage.

Reliable operations also depend on reliable information. I worked with the relevant teams to restore Matomo analytics visibility, investigate an incorrect completed-shipment status and improve UK product cross-reference information. Each change addressed a different symptom, but the method was consistent: validate the evidence, identify the responsible hand-off and make the next action clear to the team able to resolve it.

  • Matomo reporting restored
  • Completed-shipment status issue investigated and corrected
  • UK product cross-reference improved

Outcome and channel evidence

A more dependable channel, within a wider team effort.

During the period I managed the channel, online order share increased from 25.83% to 36.64%, while online value share rose from 16.25% to 23.15%. The UK became the second-highest-utilisation market globally and the highest in Europe across more than 20 markets.

These results reflect wider commercial and team activity. My contribution has been to make the channel more dependable in daily use: identifying issues earlier, connecting operational evidence across systems and turning recurring friction into clearer processes and actionable change.

  • +10.81pponline order share during my tenure
  • +6.90pponline value share during my tenure

The practice behind the work

The medium changed.
The underlying practice did not.

Across flowers, spaces and systems, I return to the same working practice: understand the whole situation, give ambiguity a useful structure and stay accountable until the result works.

01

See the whole picture

Observe closely. Understand the relationships between people, systems, space and information before acting.

02

Give ambiguity a structure

Explore what could be, make a judgement and translate an unclear need into a brief, process, journey or plan that others can use.

03

Make it real

Carry it through. Coordinate the details, test the result and stay accountable until the experience works. Then look again.
